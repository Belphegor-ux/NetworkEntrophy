Iterative re-estimation
of edge-criticality metrics

Every removal reshapes the graph — so a one-shot ranking drifts out of alignment with the network it is dismantling. We recompute the metric after each edge removal, and ask when it pays off.

Why It Matters

The edges that hold a network together are worth finding before they fail

Critical links

A few edges sustain connectivity between otherwise-separate regions. Locating them pre-empts cascading collapse in power, transport, and communication networks.

Edges are under-studied

Robustness research has long centred on critical nodes. The complementary edge-removal problem stays comparatively under-explored.

Static rankings go stale

Most indices score edges from one view of the intact graph. After the first cut, that ordering already describes a network that no longer exists.

The Idea

Borrow the iterative principle that sharpened Link Entropy methods

Deep Link Entropy and Improved Link Entropy both gained efficiency by recomputing after every removal. We apply that same step-by-step re-estimation to three local metrics of increasing sophistication — and report honestly where it helps and where it doesn't.

01 · local degree

LDC

Link Degree Centrality — purely local degree product.

02 · local paths

LLBCe

Link-Local Betweenness — edge betweenness on the edge's first-order domain.

03 · + entropy

LLBMEe1

Mapping-entropy extension — couples local betweenness with neighbourhood diversity.

Methods · Three Metrics

From a degree product to an entropy-weighted local betweenness

LDC(e) = ku · kv
degree product · remove descending · O(|E|)
LLBCe(e) = 1 / [|Γ|(|Γ|−1)] · Σs,t∈Γ(e) σst(e)/σst ,  Γ(e)={u,v}∪N(u)∪N(v)
local edge betweenness on the first-order central domain · geodesics counted in the FULL graph (no subgraph cutting) · remove descending
LLBMEe1(e₁) = −LLBCeraw(e₁) · Σe₂∈N(e₁) log LLBCeraw(e₂)
inverted criticality — most negative = most critical · remove ascending

Methods · The Loop

Recompute → remove the top edge → repeat

  • 1. Compute the metric for every edge of the current graph.
  • 2. Rank, and remove the single most-critical edge.
  • 3. Record giant-component size & fraction removed.
  • 4. Repeat on the reduced graph until no edges remain.
Efficiency criterion
AUC = ∫₀¹ Rgc(ρ) dρ
trapezoidal · lower AUC = critical edges removed earlier = better ranking

Rgc normalised by node count, ρ by edge count — so AUC is comparable across networks of different sizes.

Cost: the iterative variant re-evaluates the metric |E| times — the trade-off we quantify.

Findings

Three clean takeaways

+32.7%
mean · up to +43.7%

LLBMEe1 gains most

Both betweenness and neighbourhood entropy shift sharply during dismantling, so a one-shot ranking goes stale fastest — improves on every network.

+23.3%
mean · all 4 networks

LLBCe gains consistently

Lowers AUC on every network; biggest wins on dense graphs (incl. near-complete Baseball, where iteration breaks the static ties).

±
topology-dependent

LDC: conditional

Helps on sparse graphs (Transport +18%, Dolphins +3%) but slightly degrades on dense Jazz & Baseball — an honest negative result, not suppressed.

Interpretation

Topology governs how much iteration is worth

Dense & clustered → small gain

High clustering (Jazz C=0.62, Baseball C=0.98) provides local redundancy that blunts the effect of any single removal — the static ranking stays serviceable.

Sparse & bridge-led → large gain

The near-tree Transport graph (C=0.03) fragments abruptly and rewards a ranking that continually re-targets each newly-exposed cut edge.

The lone exception: on the near-complete Baseball graph the betweenness metrics tie statically — and iteration is uniquely able to disambiguate them.

Threats to Validity

What we are careful about

  • Computational cost. Iteration recomputes the metric after every removal — multiplying work by |E|, with local shortest-path counting inside each step. It scales far less favourably than static ranking.
  • LLBMEe1 sign convention. The inverted-criticality log-sum can flip sign if every neighbour's raw subset-betweenness exceeds unity; we monitor it, but it did not alter ranking order on these four networks.
  • Scope. Four benchmark networks spanning a wide topological range — but unit-weight, topology-only; no electrical/capacity model.
Conclusion

Adaptive rankings win —
when the topology lets them.

Iterative re-estimation robustly sharpens the two betweenness-based metrics on every network, and helps the degree product specifically on sparse, bridge-dominated graphs. The benefit tracks sparsity — and we report the two cases where it doesn't.
