Kirchhoff Flow Criticality A fast, density-adaptive way to find the most critical lines in a power grid — so we know what to protect first.

current-flow throughput effective-resistance irreplaceability auto-tunes to graph density

The problem

  • A grid has hundreds of lines — but a few matter far more than the rest.
  • Lose the wrong one and the network splits in two.
  • We want to rank every line by how critical it is, so operators protect the important ones first (N-1 planning).

The idea: two electrical signals

signal 1
Throughput (current-flow)

How much electricity routes through this line when the grid is treated as a resistor network. High = a busy corridor.

signal 2
Irreplaceability (effective resistance)

How few alternative paths exist. A bridge = 1.0 (no backup); a well-meshed line → 0.

Both come free from a single matrix solve. KFC multiplies them.

The formula

KFC(e)  =  cf1(e)  ×  ( 1 / (1 − Reff(e)) )β
  • cf1 = throughput  ·  Reff = irreplaceability
  • β auto-adapts to how tree-like the graph is:
β = clip( 1 − mean(Reff) / 0.15 , 0, 1 )

β = 0 on sparse grids (like Tokyo)
→ KFC = fast current-flow, exactly. Never worse.

β → 1 on dense networks (like Jazz)
→ irreplaceability weighting turns on. A small win.

The honest takeaway

≈0.1%

KFC's average accuracy gain over plain current-flow — small, and only on the densest network.

  • Real win: the algorithmic speed-up (fast current-flow) — 6–13× faster, works everywhere.
  • KFC's guarantee: it never does worse than current-flow, and adapts upward when the graph is dense.
  • Not oversold: no fusion decisively beats current-flow on these 4 networks. More networks needed to prove more.

Why it helps — and what's next

Use it to…
  • Prioritise which lines to protect (N-1 planning)
  • Decide where to add redundancy — re-score to see criticality drop
  • Re-run instantly after any topology change (~15 ms)
